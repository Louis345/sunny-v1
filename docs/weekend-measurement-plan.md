# Weekend measurement plan (2026-11-13 to 11-16)

The kids are away. The fun side works. This weekend Sunny learns to measure itself against reality, and the
measurements decide what content gets built next.

## The two questions

1. **Can Sunny teach?** Grounded in school grades, not in Sunny's own game scores.
2. **Can she do the assignment alone, in silence?** The original worksheet, no companion, no hints, one try, and
   ideally on paper.

Behind both: does it stick (days and weeks later), and is she stronger than before she used Sunny?

## The plan, in order of return on effort

1. **Grades that arrive late.** Teachers return tests 1–3 weeks later. Each week's predictions stay locked with
   their homework week. A grade uploaded on any later date is matched to that week and scored then. The report shows
   "waiting for grade, N days" for weeks still open. First check that `sunny:homework:upload` accepts an old week.
2. **Test mode (silent check).** A "check like school" stop: the original assignment items as printed, one try, no
   companion, no hints, no sounds (spelling: dictation only), with rewards after the check rather than during it.
   Saved as its own kind of evidence (`school_format`). The Planner can place it (for example the day before a
   test), and a parent can launch it.
3. **Paper mode (transfer).** Print the worksheet or a fresh version of it. She does it in pencil, a parent takes a
   photo, and Sunny grades it with vision. The gap between game score and paper score is the transfer gap.
4. **Retention checks.** Every new board quietly includes 2–3 cold items from 1, 2 and 4 weeks ago, with no help.
   This answers "does it stick" per child and per skill.
5. **"Is Sunny working?" page, one per child.** School grade vs Sunny's prediction, test-mode and paper scores,
   retention over time, own vs helped trend, and cost per session. Red flag: a high game score next to a low
   paper or school score.
6. **Then the intervention scoreboard.** Each teaching strategy (a cue, a mnemonic, a picture) recorded per item,
   with its outcome a week later compared against items without it. Measurements drive the content.
