### Top 5 fixes, ranked by return on the girls' learning

1. **Make sure her work is saved (finding 1).** Every other part of Sunny runs on saved answers. This cost Reina a full session the day before a test, it is the cheapest fix here, and it can happen again to either girl after any hand repair of the data.
2. **Built games must say how they are played, and the companion must be able to check in (findings 2, 3, 4).** This is the wall Ila hits. It cost her 15 of 35 minutes on 11/10 and Reina 12 minutes on 11/09. Three small jobs: the build check, the facts on each turn, the check-in tool.
3. **Let Reina meet a problem she has not seen, and record when she was helped (findings 7, 8).** Without these Sunny cannot find her limit: it has no harder problem to offer and no way to tell her own work from Matilda's.
4. **Give the Planner tonight's retro with true counts (findings 5, 6, 14).** The Planner's self-review is the strongest part of Sunny. It is reasoning from a late retro, a "zero right" that is false, and a reward call filed as a bad lesson.
5. **Give Sunny a memory for its games (findings 9, 10).** True counts, time to first answer, last week's games, and a keep / fix / retire note the builder reads. This is how a game she understood comes back with new words, and how the one that walled her does not.

### Two-minute summary for the parent

**What is working.** The Planner really does review itself. Its journal names what it tried, quotes what she wrote, and changes course; it even withdrew its own wrong diagnosis for Reina without being told. The retro, timeline and scorecard added on 11/09 are the right instruments. Ila went from never spelling "ornament" alone to getting it right on her own on 11/09.

**Where Ila stands.** Her test is Friday 11/13. On her own she is 0 of 5 on dormant, 0 of 3 on carnivore and 0 of 3 on pardon. On 11/10 all 13 of her counted answers were with help, in a session where a parent sat beside her and Elli spelled M-A-N-T aloud in six separate turns. Her biggest cost is not the words. It is the wall: 15 minutes in a game nobody explained, with a companion telling her to type in a game that wanted taps, and a frustration detector that could not see that game.

**Where Reina stands.** She has answered every problem Sunny has, so Sunny has nothing harder to give her and has removed her Quest and Boss. Her record says she did everything alone, which is not true. And the one session where she visibly struggled, which is where her limit is, was not saved.

**The pattern behind most findings.** Sunny writes many notes, and several of them disagree with each other while nothing compares them: answers given versus answers saved, the game's count versus the chart's count, the retro's "no help recorded" versus the chart's "with help", the Planner's story for a stop versus what the built game actually asks her to do. Most fixes above are one of two kinds: give an AI a fact it does not have, or give it a tool to act on a fact it does have. None needs a hard-coded rule.

**What the parent still does by hand.** On 11/09 the owner closed two boards early to redirect the Planner, edited a game file, and sat in on the evening session. On 11/10 Reina, not Sunny, reported that saving was broken. The first two fixes in the top 5 are the ones that most reduce that.
